Playing songs without being a musician: your first song without reading a note

2026-07-29 · ChordPlai

Most people who want to make music hit the same wall: learning an instrument takes months. Your fingertips hurt, chord changes don't land, and reading music is a whole other language. Most people quit right there — not because they lack musical talent, but because the distance between the tool and the desire is simply too long.

There is a way to shorten that distance: remove the tool in the middle. Show your hand to the camera and play the chord directly.

What do you actually need in order to play a song?

Accompanying a song doesn't take technique. It takes three simple decisions:

  1. Which key? Wherever your voice feels comfortable. If you're unsure, start with A minor or C major.
  2. Which chords? Most popular songs revolve around four chords. Knowing those four unlocks hundreds of songs.
  3. When do they change? Usually every four beats. The metronome counts that for you.

Notice what's not on that list: no reading music, no finger technique, no 10,000 hours.

The four-chord secret

A surprisingly large chunk of Western popular music revolves around the same four-chord pattern. Written in the language of degrees, it looks like this: I – V – vi – IV. In the world of A minor, that's Am – F – C – G.

If you can play those four in order, you can sing dozens of familiar songs over them. And because you learned the pattern as degrees, if it sits too high for your voice you just change the key and keep playing with the exact same hand movements.

Playing with your hands: first song in 10 minutes

In ChordPlai's Air Chord mode, the number of fingers you hold up picks the degree of the chord. All you have to learn is a handful of hand signs:

  • ☝️ One finger — 1st degree: the song's "home", the chord that settles everything
  • 🖐️ Open palm — 5th degree: tension, the chord that wants to go home
  • 🤘 Horns — 6th degree: wistful, emotional color
  • 4️⃣ Four fingers — 4th degree: opening up, a feeling of lift
  • ✊ Fist — silence

Learning these five moves takes ten minutes and lasts a lifetime. Getting those same four chords to ring cleanly on a guitar takes weeks.

A recipe for your first session

  1. Open the site, allow camera access, and choose A minor as your scale.
  2. Set the metronome to 70 BPM — calm, and easy to sing over.
  3. Cycle through this sequence, changing every four beats: ☝️ → 🤘 → 4️⃣ → 🖐️
  4. Just listen for two rounds. On the third, start humming "la la".
  5. Once the melody settles, try the lyrics of a song you know — most of them will fit.

"But is this real music?"

The people who ask this question most are the ones who haven't started yet. Real music is being able to pass a feeling to a listener; the tool you use to do it is secondary. A piano is a machine too, and so is an electric guitar. A camera and hand tracking are simply the newest additions to the list.

What's more: by playing in degrees you end up learning real music theory without noticing. If you decide to pick up a guitar one day, your ear will already be ready.

So what should you do today?

Give it five minutes. Turn on the camera, try the four signs, hum something over them, and record it. When you listen back tomorrow you'll be surprised — because for the first time, you'll have moved "someday I'll make music" into the past tense.
