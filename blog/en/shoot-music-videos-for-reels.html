Shooting a music video without an instrument: a practical guide for Reels

2026-07-29 · ChordPlai

Most people who want to make music content get stuck in the same place: just singing to a camera is visually static, and you don't know how to play an instrument. Playing chords with your hands fills exactly that gap — it's a performance you both hear and see.

Why do hand gestures work so well on video?

Viewers scan the first second of a video asking "wait, what's happening here?" Hands moving through the air with sound changing in sync creates that question instantly. Curiosity is the strongest fuel for watch time, and watch time is the only thing algorithms reward.

Framing: vertical or horizontal?

  • Reels / TikTok / Shorts: Vertical 9:16. In this mode ChordPlai centers the camera and adds branded bands above and below — your hands and face stay visible at the same time.
  • YouTube: Horizontal 16:9. A wider frame, and the interface elements are easier to read.

While you record, the name of the chord playing, the drum pattern and the hand skeleton overlay are burned into the video too. Those layers give viewers the sense that there's a real system at work here.

Light and background

  1. Put your main light source in front of your face; if there's a window behind you, close the curtains.
  2. Keep the background plain — a flat wall is best. A busy background splits both the hand tracking and your viewer's attention.
  3. Make sure there's contrast between the back of your hand and the background; hands read beautifully against a dark wall.

A structure that holds viewers for 15 seconds

  1. 0–2 s: Have the sound already playing as you raise your hand. A silent opening loses people.
  2. 2–5 s: Change a degree — let the viewer realize the sound is tied to your hand.
  3. 5–10 s: Start singing. At this point curiosity hands over to the music.
  4. 10–15 s: Tilt your wrist to flip the chord major or minor — a small but impressive closing trick.

Choosing chords: which patterns land best?

Complex progressions get lost in a short video. Pick a four-chord loop and play it twice through. A pattern that's proven itself in A minor: ☝️ → 🤘 → 4️⃣ → 🖐️. Vocals: you won't hear them live, but they land beautifully in the recording

In ChordPlai your vocal isn't played back live through the speakers (which prevents mic feedback), but it goes into the recording fully processed: EQ, compression, reverb and scale-aware harmonies. So when you watch the recording, your voice will sound much fuller than you expected.

🎧 Tip: When the recording ends, hit "Download" and listen to the video on headphones. Shooting the same performance twice with different harmonizer modes quickly shows you which color suits you.

When you publish

  • Explain what you did in one sentence in the first comment — people will be asking "how is this happening?"
  • Make the first frame the moment your hand is in the air; the cover frame has to create curiosity.
  • Produce a series with the same pattern in different scales — repeating a format that works is the fastest way to grow.

Gear list

A laptop with a camera. That's it. No microphone, no interface, no instrument, no lighting kit — and if you want better sound, a headset mic is plenty.
